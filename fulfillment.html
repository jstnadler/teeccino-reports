Day-of-Week Pattern
Typical = the middle value for that weekday over the 12 weeks, so one catch-up day (like the Tuesday after a holiday) does not distort it. Only days the warehouse shipped are counted.

There is a weekly rhythm. Tuesday is the heaviest day (typically 182 orders) and Friday the lightest (typically 149) — a gap of about 32 orders. Part of the day-to-day swing is this weekly pattern; the rest is ordinary variation, which is why the weekly total is the steadier number to plan by.
How to read it: gold bars are orders fulfilled each week; the blue line is orders placed that week; the shaded band is the usual range and the dashed line the typical week. “Usual range” is the middle half of the 12 weeks (25th to 75th percentile), so by construction about a quarter of weeks land above it and a quarter below. Holiday weeks are kept in, marked with an asterisk: week of Sep 7 (4 shipping days, 959 orders). Orders placed over a holiday do not go away, so a short week is not a light week.

Daily Detail — Last 60 Days
The figures behind the chart above, one row per calendar day (60 days, 41 of them shipping days and 19 closed). Closed days are shown by default — a day the warehouse did not ship is the reason a gap exists, so hiding it turns the gap into an unexplained jump in the date column. Order counts are DTC + Target + Walmart (the team’s workload); the SLA columns are DTC only (the service promise). They are different populations on purpose — a figure in one group does not reconcile against a figure in the other. Orders Placed is the same channel scope as the shipped columns (DTC + Target + Walmart), counted on the day the order came in — so it is the demand that arrived against the work that went out. It is deliberately still shown on closed days: weekend orders do not vanish, they queue up and become Monday. Orders Placed is complete through 2026-10-26 — the order feed lands a day behind the shipping feed, so the 1 newer day(s) show a dash rather than a zero. A dash there means not in yet, not nobody ordered.

Why the stale group is shown but not counted as backlog. 1,174 orders ($37,698) have no shipment record and are more than 30 days old. They are not orders the warehouse is about to pick, and folding them into the headline would publish about a thousand phantom open orders. The two largest causes are identified above and counted, not estimated: Walmart WFS orders ship from Walmart’s own warehouse and can never acquire one of our shipment records, and the 2025-12 cluster is the known December order-number match failure. The remainder is labelled unattributed rather than quietly absorbed into one of the named causes. They are shown because a number withheld without the reader knowing it was withheld is worse than a number with a caveat.
