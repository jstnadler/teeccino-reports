Daily Detail — Last 60 Days
The figures behind the chart above, one row per calendar day (60 days, 43 of them shipping days and 17 closed). Closed days are shown by default — a day the warehouse did not ship is the reason a gap exists, so hiding it turns the gap into an unexplained jump in the date column. Order counts are DTC + Target + Walmart (the team’s workload); the SLA columns are DTC only (the service promise). They are different populations on purpose — a figure in one group does not reconcile against a figure in the other. Orders Placed is the same channel scope as the shipped columns (DTC + Target + Walmart), counted on the day the order came in — so it is the demand that arrived against the work that went out. It is deliberately still shown on closed days: weekend orders do not vanish, they queue up and become Monday. Orders Placed is complete through 2026-09-17 — the order feed lands a day behind the shipping feed, so the 1 newer day(s) show a dash rather than a zero. A dash there means not in yet, not nobody ordered.

Click a column header to sort. Tick Shipping days only to drop the closed days; the row count above then names how many were withheld. Days with no shipments show a dash, not a zero — the warehouse ships business days only, and a closed Saturday rendered as “0 orders, 0% on time” reads as a failure instead of a weekend. A row marked in progress is the current ship day: it is still being worked, its averages are a partial read, and it is shown so the day is not mistaken for missing. Orders Placed counts orders by the day they were placed, not by the day they shipped, so it will not tie out to the shipped columns on any single row — an order placed Sunday ships Monday. Over a full window the two converge, minus whatever is still in the queue.

Age is counted in shipping days, not calendar days, and the shipping calendar is read from the shipments themselves rather than assumed to be Mon–Fri — so a weekend and a holiday the warehouse actually took are both handled without anyone maintaining a list. The newest ship day is excluded from the count because it is still being worked: an order placed Friday reads “not yet due” on Monday morning, which is what the warehouse would say, rather than “1 day late”.

Why the stale group is shown but not counted as backlog. 1,052 orders ($36,693) have no shipment record and are more than 30 days old. They are not orders the warehouse is about to pick, and folding them into the headline would publish about a thousand phantom open orders. The two largest causes are identified above and counted, not estimated: Walmart WFS orders ship from Walmart’s own warehouse and can never acquire one of our shipment records, and the 2025-12 cluster is the known December order-number match failure. The remainder is labelled unattributed rather than quietly absorbed into one of the named causes. They are shown because a number withheld without the reader knowing it was withheld is worse than a number with a caveat.
