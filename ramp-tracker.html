What this shows. Read it as: "if a customer cost me $X to acquire, how long until they've earned that $X back in profit?" The X-axis is your acquisition cost (nCPA); the Y-axis is months until that customer's cumulative gross profit climbs back up to what you paid. The marker is where your current cost sits.

To find payback for any nCPA, you ask "when does cumulative profit cross that dollar amount?" — e.g. nCPA $20 → <1 mo, $35 → ~5 mo, $46 → ~12 mo, $55 → never. As cost creeps toward the ~$55 ceiling you need almost their whole lifetime to break even, so the line goes near-vertical — and past ~$55 they never pay back (the line just stops).
How $55 is derived: 2024 cohorts, lifetime-to-date averaged $99 net revenue per customer to date × 55% gross margin ≈ $55. It's "lifetime-to-date" (~18–24 months observed, not infinite) and a blend — ~2 orders & ~9% subscribers on average, so a loyal repeat-buyer is worth more and a one-and-done less. For a scaling call the blended average is the right number: the next ad dollar buys that whole mix.

New vs. returning — why early payback is slow. A customer's first order is their least profitable one: a new customer's first order yields only ~$21 gross profit vs ~$31 for a returning order (mostly smaller starter baskets, not a different margin rate). So the profit that pays back your nCPA leans on the repeat tail, not order one. That sets the reading line: nCPA below the $21 first-order line is cash-positive on order one (recouped before they ever reorder); above it, you're floating the cost until they come back — the further above, the more reorders you're banking on.

Plan caveats. Spend level is anchored to known-profitable history, not a fitted response curve (a monthly spend↔new regression is too noisy — spend & demand co-move with promos/season). The efficiency half of the blend is still partly contaminated by diminishing returns — historically low-spend months (e.g. Dec) look extra-efficient partly because we under-spent them and were on the steep part of the curve. The revenue half deliberately tempers that (Dec is a low-revenue month, so the geo-mean pulls it back). Treat the blended index as directional, not a guarantee. Payback comes from the LTV→GP curve at the expected cost/customer. Proj. net rev is spend-responsive: new-customer revenue (the new customers this spend buys × ~$39 net per first order) + a seasonal returning-customer base (prior-year same-month repeat revenue × 4% YoY). So flexing spend up lifts the new-revenue half; the returning base is locked in from prior cohorts. MER = proj net ÷ spend. Execution (Meta/Google split, campaign structure) sits with the agency.
