How to read this. Your average nCPA (spend ÷ all new customers) is what the MTD dashboard shows — it rises slowly because cheap early customers drag it down. The marginal nCPA (extra spend ÷ the extra customers it bought) is the price of the next customers as you scale — it rises fast and crosses the $48 break-even line first. Watch the marginal line: the moment it pokes above $48 while your average still looks fine, that's the signal to stop scaling.

Projected Payback by nCPA

What this shows. How many months until an acquired customer's cumulative gross profit recoups what you paid to get them. Payback rises gently as nCPA climbs — then goes near-vertical around $55, the most a customer is worth over their life. Past that, they never pay back. The marker is where your current cost sits.

New vs. returning. The acquisition order is the lowest-margin one — a new customer's first order yields ~$21 gross profit vs ~$31 for a returning order (mostly smaller baskets, not margin rate). So early payback leans on the repeat tail: nCPA below the $21 first-order line is cash-positive on order one; above it, you're floating the cost until they reorder.

Honesty notes. (1) Week-over-week marginal is noisy (promo timing, seasonality, day-mix) — trust the 4-week rolling marginal as the headline, not single-week jumps. (2) Baseline-monitoring mode: no ramp set. Marginal ≈ average at normal spend; the gap opens once you scale. Set RAMP_START in the builder when the test begins. (4) The cleanest causal read of marginal nCPA is a geo holdout; this tracker infers it from spend/customer deltas, which is directional.
