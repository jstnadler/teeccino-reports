Cost & value of a customer

nCPA (cost per new customer)
What we pay in ads to win one new customer: total ad spend ÷ new customers. The headline light tracks this vs what a customer is worth.
A new customer is worth ($42)
Blended 2-year contribution margin per new customer — the break-even line. Below it, acquisition adds profit.
A subscriber is worth ($107)
2-year CM when the first order is a subscription (~2.5× the blend) — 6 orders / 82% repeat. Shows the prize for converting first orders to subscription.
First-order break-even ($14)
CM on a new customer's first order alone. nCPA below this = cash-positive on order one; above it, you recoup over the repeat tail.
Net revenue
subtotal − discounts (excludes shipping & tax) — the realized sales value the customer paid for product. The base of CM and projected revenue below.
COGS
Cost of goods sold — the product cost per order from the COGS table.
Shipping cost
Actual per-order carrier cost (ShipStation) for 2024-onward orders; a ~$7.50 estimate for the small pre-2024 tail.
Contribution margin (CM)
Net revenue − COGS − shipping cost — the same lean definition as the Monthly KPI. Ad spend is the separate CAC, not subtracted here.
DTC
Direct-to-consumer — teeccino.com orders only. Wholesale (Walmart, Target, Faire) is excluded everywhere on this page.

Payback & efficiency

Payback
Months until a new customer's cumulative CM recoups the nCPA we paid, read off the LTV→CM curve. Past the $42 2-yr ceiling it never pays back.
Profit / order
Lean CM per order after product, shipping & ad cost — the weekly per-order contribution.
MER
Marketing Efficiency Ratio = projected net revenue ÷ ad spend. A sanity check, not a target.
Ad % of revenue (TACOS)
Ad spend as a percent of revenue. Lower = more efficient; it's just MER inverted. Trailing-12-mo actual is in the stat at top.

What this shows. Read it as: "if a customer cost me $X to acquire, how long until they've earned that $X back in profit?" The X-axis is your acquisition cost (nCPA); the Y-axis is months until that customer's cumulative contribution margin climbs back up to what you paid. The marker is where your current cost sits.

Where the curve comes from. A customer's contribution margin piles up fast early, then flattens as their value reaches ~$42 blended lifetime CM (mature 2024-and-earlier cohorts, contribution margin):

To find payback for any nCPA, you ask "when does cumulative CM cross that dollar amount?" — e.g. nCPA $15 → <1 mo, $25 → ~6 mo, $35 → ~15 mo, $42 → ~24 mo. As cost creeps toward the ~$42 ceiling you need almost their whole 2-year life to break even, so the line goes near-vertical — and past ~$42 they never pay back (the line just stops).
How $42 is derived: mature 2024-and-earlier cohorts, contribution margin — cumulative contribution margin per new customer over 2 years (net revenue − COGS − shipping cost — the same lean definition as the Monthly KPI dashboard; ad spend is the separate CAC, not subtracted here). Same lean CM definition as the Monthly KPI, the cohort grid and the 2-Year LTV page. It's a blend — ~2 orders & ~9% subscribers on average, so a loyal repeat-buyer is worth more and a one-and-done less. For a scaling call the blended average is the right number: the next ad dollar buys that whole mix.

New vs. returning — why early payback is slow. A customer's first order is their least profitable one: a new customer's first order yields only ~$14 contribution margin vs ~$19 for a returning order (mostly smaller starter baskets, not a different margin rate). So the margin that pays back your nCPA leans on the repeat tail, not order one. That sets the reading line: nCPA below the $14 first-order line is cash-positive on order one (recouped before they ever reorder); above it, you're floating the cost until they come back — the further above, the more reorders you're banking on.

Plan caveats. The spend floor is the budgeted monthly figure from the 2026 forecast; the +25% upside is a deliberate lean-in, gated by the live new-customer-cost light, not a fitted response curve (a monthly spend↔new regression is too noisy — spend & demand co-move with promos/season). Expected new customers and payback assume efficiency holds as spend rises toward the upside; if nCPA climbs, take less of it. Payback comes from the LTV→GP curve at the expected cost/customer. Proj. net rev is spend-responsive: new-customer revenue (the new customers this spend buys × ~$40 net per first order) + a seasonal returning-customer base (prior-year same-month repeat revenue × 4% YoY). So flexing spend up lifts the new-revenue half; the returning base is locked in from prior cohorts. MER = proj net ÷ spend. Execution (Meta/Google split, campaign structure) sits with the agency.
