Projected Payback by nCPA

What this shows. Read it as: "if a customer cost me $X to acquire, how long until they've earned that $X back in profit?" The X-axis is your acquisition cost (nCPA); the Y-axis is months until that customer's cumulative contribution margin climbs back up to what you paid. The marker is where your current cost sits.

Where the curve comes from. A customer's contribution margin piles up fast early, then flattens as their value reaches ~$47 blended lifetime CM (mature 2024-and-earlier cohorts, contribution margin):

To find payback for any nCPA, you ask "when does cumulative CM cross that dollar amount?" — e.g. nCPA $15 → <1 mo, $25 → ~4 mo, $35 → ~10 mo, $47 → ~24 mo. As cost creeps toward the ~$47 ceiling you need almost their whole 2-year life to break even, so the line goes near-vertical — and past ~$47 they never pay back (the line just stops).
How $47 is derived: mature 2024-and-earlier cohorts, contribution margin — cumulative contribution margin per new customer over 2 years (net revenue + shipping revenue collected − COGS − shipping cost − card processing; customers pay shipping under $59, which offsets the carrier cost). Same CM definition as the cohort grid and the 2-Year LTV page. It's a blend — ~2 orders & ~9% subscribers on average, so a loyal repeat-buyer is worth more and a one-and-done less. For a scaling call the blended average is the right number: the next ad dollar buys that whole mix.

New vs. returning — why early payback is slow. A customer's first order is their least profitable one: a new customer's first order yields only ~$18 contribution margin vs ~$27 for a returning order (mostly smaller starter baskets, not a different margin rate). So the margin that pays back your nCPA leans on the repeat tail, not order one. That sets the reading line: nCPA below the $18 first-order line is cash-positive on order one (recouped before they ever reorder); above it, you're floating the cost until they come back — the further above, the more reorders you're banking on.

Plan caveats. Spend level is anchored to known-profitable history, not a fitted response curve (a monthly spend↔new regression is too noisy — spend & demand co-move with promos/season). The efficiency half of the blend is still partly contaminated by diminishing returns — historically low-spend months (e.g. Dec) look extra-efficient partly because we under-spent them and were on the steep part of the curve. The revenue half deliberately tempers that (Dec is a low-revenue month, so the geo-mean pulls it back). Treat the blended index as directional, not a guarantee. Payback comes from the LTV→GP curve at the expected cost/customer. Proj. net rev is spend-responsive: new-customer revenue (the new customers this spend buys × ~$39 net per first order) + a seasonal returning-customer base (prior-year same-month repeat revenue × 4% YoY). So flexing spend up lifts the new-revenue half; the returning base is locked in from prior cohorts. MER = proj net ÷ spend. Execution (Meta/Google split, campaign structure) sits with the agency.
