Read this first — what “approve” means here

Justin ruled on 2026-08-05 that no Amazon ads or listings change may proceed without your approval, and that approvals get collected here in your own portal rather than buried in a chat thread. This page is that mechanism.

Nothing on this page is a button. There is no programmatic write path to Amazon — verified 2026-08-05: no mutation code exists in any pipeline script, and there has been zero campaign status or budget churn from our side. Every agent in the Amazon pod is advisory. Clicking, reading, or agreeing with a card on this page does not pause a campaign, change a bid, or touch a listing.

What approval actually sets in motion:

  1. You authorize it — tell Justin or AmazonSmith which cards are a yes.
  2. Justin releases any spend separately. Approving a card never releases budget; that is a second, explicit decision and only Justin makes it.
  3. A human executes it in Seller Central — usually you. Each card below spells out the literal clicks, or names the agent who prepares the playbook first.
  4. The card's status changes here from Awaiting Burke to Approved → Done (or Rejected), so there is one place that says what was decided and whether it actually happened.

Money figures are measured from our own Amazon data unless a card explicitly says estimate or forecast.

Open decisions — highest money at stake first

Each card states what changes, the measured number behind it, what it is worth, who proposed it, what happens on approval, and what it costs to do nothing. The figures sit in several different units — gross revenue, contribution margin, ad spend, and return per dollar spent — so each headline number is labelled with its unit rather than plotted on a shared bar.

Needs a human, not an approval — 2 items

These are not decisions and there is nothing here to approve or reject. They are two pieces of information we cannot get from the data, and both are currently blocking sharper answers above.

1. Burke — check Seller Central for anything dated 2026-05-20

The Buy Box suppression in card 2 has a single, sharp onset date. Our data can see the effect but not the cause; the cause, if there is an announced one, will be sitting in Seller Central. Please look in three places for anything dated 2026-05-20:

  • Pricing Health → “Featured Offer Ineligible”
  • Account Health → Product Policy Compliance
  • Performance Notifications (the inbox, including anything filed/archived)
Finding nothing is itself the answer. If none of those three carry anything dated 2026-05-20, that effectively rules out a policy action against us and points instead at an unannounced change to Amazon's pricing reference — a very different problem with a very different fix. So please report back either way; a clean “nothing there” is a useful result, not a dead end.

2. Finance — fresh Acctivate item-cost export, including the Unit column

Every margin figure on this page rests on unit costs, and our cost table has holes. 20.4% of Amazon gross — $524,564 — has no cost row at all, so roughly a fifth of the revenue we are making decisions about is running on incomplete costing.

The export must include the Unit column. Without it we cannot tell a case cost from an each cost, and a cost row we can't interpret is worse than a missing one — it produces a confident wrong margin instead of an obvious gap.

Until this lands, treat every margin and contribution number on this page as directionally right but not final, and treat the coverage gap as the reason a card is ranked by measured ad efficiency rather than by full landed profit.

This gap is already load-bearing on two cards. 45001-L (card 5) has no cost row at all, so that card rests on ACOS against a blended break-even rather than on its own margin. 10031L and 10051L (card 3) are in the same position, which is why that card's headline is stated as revenue per ad dollar and not as contribution.
