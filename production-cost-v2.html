📏 How we count — cases and units

UNITone individual retail item — a single bag, box or canister a customer buys. The smallest thing we make. Every “per unit” figure on this page means exactly this.
CASEone shipping case, made of units. How many is the units / case column in every table — e.g. a case of loose 11 oz French Roast is 24 units; a 6-pack case is 6 units.
All cases are made of units:  units = cases × units/case  ·  $/case = $/unit × units/case.  Both hold exactly, in every section.

Every section has a Per case / Per unit switch. It changes only the basis the numbers are stated in — never which runs are included. Production is scheduled and counted by the case, so Per case is the default. Plant-wide totals (Sections 1, 1a, 3a, 3b) count every item that ran, including component tea bags, which are units in their own right — use the per-SKU sections for like-for-like comparison.

Reading a work order that spans several lines

A work order regularly takes more than one line on the sheet — a break splits it, or it runs past the end of the day and finishes the next. That is normal paperwork, and it changes how the lines read:

plan
Planned quantity is repeated on every line, not divided between them. So a single line is never “x% of plan” on its own.
actual
Quantity, hours and downtime are split across the lines and have to be added up to get the job.
setup
Setup lands on the first line, so its labor per unit looks high in isolation and settles once the job finishes.

What the flags mean

A flag is a prompt to look at the row, not a verdict. Some are legitimate — a genuinely short run, a one-off rework. The point is that you no longer have to find them by eye.

Two things are never flagged, because they are not problems. Washing and cleaning — a blender wash, an air clean-out, a tooling change, a “go to Assembly” — books hours and makes nothing by design. And a job dated today has not finished — and one dated next week has not run yet, since the schedule is written about three weeks ahead — so of course neither has made anything. Both are labelled in the Row is and Job state columns and filterable above, so they stay visible and countable; they just are not accused of anything. A run sheet inside a 10-day window that has not been filled in yet is treated the same way.
