Daily Detail — October 2026
The figures behind the chart above, one row per calendar day (28 days, 20 of them shipping days and 8 closed). Closed days are shown by default — a day the warehouse did not ship is the reason a gap exists, so hiding it turns the gap into an unexplained jump in the date column. Order counts are DTC + Target + Walmart (the team’s workload); the SLA columns are DTC only (the service promise). They are different populations on purpose — a figure in one group does not reconcile against a figure in the other. Orders Placed is the same channel scope as the shipped columns (DTC + Target + Walmart), counted on the day the order came in — so it is the demand that arrived against the work that went out. It is deliberately still shown on closed days: weekend orders do not vanish, they queue up and become Monday. Orders Placed is complete through 2026-10-27 — the order feed lands a day behind the shipping feed, so the 1 newer day(s) show a dash rather than a zero. A dash there means not in yet, not nobody ordered.

Days with no shipments show a dash, not a zero — the warehouse ships business days only, and a closed Saturday rendered as “0 orders, 0% on time” reads as a failure instead of a weekend. A row marked in progress is the current ship day: it is still being worked, its averages are a partial read, and it is shown so the day is not mistaken for missing. Orders Placed counts orders by the day they were placed, not by the day they shipped, so it will not tie out to the shipped columns on any single row — an order placed Sunday ships Monday. Over a full window the two converge, minus whatever is still in the queue.
